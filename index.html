    What's live, and what's next

    Orbitry is being built in stages, and each stage is usable on its own. Here is where things honestly stand.

    Live

    Earth-orbit globe

    Every publicly tracked active satellite, positioned in your browser from current orbital elements. Search, filter by orbit type, click any object, and fast-forward time.

    In development

    Moon, Mars & deep space

    Rovers, landers, orbiters and far-flung probes like Voyager, with coordinates and mission facts drawn from NASA sources.

    Planned

    Photos, 3D models and plain-language summaries for each object, with the license and source of every asset tracked.

    Planned

    One clean JSON catalog from low Earth orbit to the surface of Mars, for educators, newsrooms and app builders.

    Planned

    Tonight's best targets for your location and gear, with warnings for satellite streaks across your frame.

    Real data, with stated limits

    A visualizer is only useful if you can trust what it shows, so here is exactly what it does and doesn't claim.

    How positions are calculated

    Positions come from public orbital elements run through the standard SGP4 model, right in your browser. That is good for visualizing and for rough pass timing, but not for collision avoidance or precise pointing.

    Orbital elements age, and accuracy drops as they do. The details panel shows how old each object's data is.

    Only publicly tracked objects appear. Some military and sensitive satellites are missing or incomplete in public data.

    Sources

    Used today: CelesTrak for orbital elements.

    Planned: NASA JPL Horizons for deep-space positions, The Space Devs' Launch Library 2 for launches, and the UCS Satellite Database and Jonathan McDowell's catalog for object details.
